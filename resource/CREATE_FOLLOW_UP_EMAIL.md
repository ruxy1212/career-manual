# LLM Instruction: Write High-Impact Job Application Follow-Up Emails

## Objective

Write a concise, professional, and impact-driven follow-up email that stands out, reaffirms technical value, and avoids generic or robotic template phrasing. 

The email should be customized to the role and the company’s engineering focus, maintaining the tone of an experienced professional rather than a desperate applicant.

---

## Overall Philosophy

A good follow-up email does not just ask "Have you read my application?" 

Instead, it:
1. Re-establishes context (role applied for, date of application).
2. Connects your specialized skillset to the company's concrete product focus.
3. Offers immediate technical value.
4. Keeps the message extremely concise and easy to read.

---

## 1. Subject Line

Ideally, send the follow-up as a reply to your initial application email (keeping the original thread). 

If sending a new thread, use a specific, clear subject line:
- Follow-up: Backend (Laravel) Engineer Application - Russell Ojegun
- Laravel Engineer Application - Russell Ojegun
- Or a value-oriented one: Scalable Laravel Architectures - Follow-up - Russell Ojegun

---

## 2. The Opening (What to Avoid)

Never start with generic, robotic, or passive phrases:
- *I am writing to follow up on my application...*
- *I hope this email finds you well...*
- *I submitted my application on [Date] and wanted to check in...*

### Instead, start with a direct, confident statement:
Identify the application and immediately pivot to why you remain aligned with their current engineering challenges.

**Formula:**
> State the position and date of application, then immediately tie it to their domain.

**Example:**
> I submitted my application for the Backend (Laravel) Engineer position on June 30th and wanted to share a brief update on why I remain highly interested in HelloMe Money's engineering direction.

---

## 3. The Core Value Reminder

Do not simply repeat your CV. Select 1 or 2 high-impact technical alignments that directly relate to the company's product line.

**Example for Fintech/Payments (Laravel):**
> Having spent my career designing secure APIs and payment integrations, I've spent a lot of time thinking about transaction reliability. I'm particularly interested in how your engineering team manages database locks and race conditions in digital wallets at scale—challenges I've resolved in my previous roles.

**Key principles for this section:**
- Refer to specific components of their product (e.g., *international payments, digital wallet infrastructure, real-time ledger systems*).
- Highlight your relevant stack (*Laravel, PHP, secure APIs, MySQL/PostgreSQL*).
- Mention outcomes or deep technical interest rather than generic "experience".

---

## 4. Low-Friction Call to Action (CTA)

Avoid open-ended or high-pressure requests:
- *Please let me know if you would like to schedule a time to speak.* (Puts the burden of work on the recruiter).
- *I look forward to hearing from you.* (Passive and generic).

### Instead, make it easy to respond:
- *My CV is attached if you need a quick reference. Let me know if a brief conversation makes sense to discuss how my backend experience aligns with your current priorities.*
- *If you need any additional code samples of secure API integrations, I'm happy to share them.*

---

## 5. Tone Checklist

Before sending, verify that the email:
- **Sounds human**: Read it aloud. Does it sound like you, or does it sound like a template?
- **Shows domain awareness**: Specifically mentions the product ecosystem (e.g., fintech, payments, wallets).
- **Stays brief**: Under 150 words. Recruiters should be able to scan it in 15 seconds.
- **Maintains confidence**: Avoids apologizing for following up.
